Top 5 Signs Your Child Needs Extra Arabic Support in MOE or CBSE Curriculum

Introduction

Is your child studying Arabic as part of the MOE or CBSE curriculum in the UAE? While Arabic is a vital subject, many students struggle with its complexities—especially non-native speakers. As parents, recognizing the early signs of difficulty can help your child get the support they need before it affects their confidence or academic performance. At Arabic Juniors, we specialize in personalized, online Arabic tuition tailored to UAE curricula for students from Grade 1 to Grade 10.

1. Falling Behind in Class or Struggling with Homework

If your child takes longer than usual to complete Arabic assignments or avoids Arabic homework altogether, it might be a red flag. Difficulty understanding instructions, vocabulary, or grammar rules are signs they could benefit from one-to-one attention in a calm, focused environment.

2. Poor Grades Despite Effort

Does your child study hard but still score low in Arabic assessments? This often means the classroom pace is too fast or the teaching style doesn't match your child's learning needs. Our certified Arabic teachers online in UAE identify and bridge these gaps through tailored lessons.

3. Lack of Confidence in Speaking or Reading Arabic

Arabic can feel intimidating, especially when students are asked to speak aloud or read in front of others. If your child avoids participation in Arabic class, it could be due to anxiety or lack of practice. Our Arabic group and one-on-one classes help children build confidence step by step.

4. Confusion About Grammar or Sentence Structure

MOE and CBSE curricula both emphasize Arabic grammar and composition. If your child is confused by sentence structures, verb forms, or even struggles to write basic answers, it's time for reinforcement. At Arabic Juniors, we simplify grammar through visuals, games, and examples.

5. Teacher Feedback or Parent Gut Feeling

Sometimes, feedback from your child’s school or your own observations is enough. If the Arabic teacher notes consistent challenges, or you notice stress around Arabic tests, don’t wait. Early intervention prevents long-term frustration and keeps your child on track.
